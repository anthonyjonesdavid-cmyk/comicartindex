## Deep dive: what makes a Jim Lee X-Men piece worth six figures?

Three of the top 10 are Jim Lee, and the spread is the story. The X-Men #6 cover brought **$437,500**, while a double-page spread from X-Men #5 brought **$52,500** in the same auction. That's the same artist and the same era, with roughly an 8x gap.

Our index shows a pattern:

- **Covers and iconic poses win.** Lee's Uncanny X-Men #268 cover (Wolverine, Captain America and Black Widow) sold for **$300,000** in 2020. Heritage called it one of the top ten covers of the early '90s, and it hadn't been on the market since the '90s ([CBR](https://www.cbr.com/jim-lee-uncanny-x-men-original-art-auctioned-300k/)).
- **A great interior page can beat a cover.** In April 2022 a single Uncanny X-Men #271 page with Wolverine, Jubilee and Psylocke sold for **$360,000**. A Savage Land spread from #274 went for **$180,000**, and a Wolverine #24 cover went for **$144,000** in the same sale ([Bleeding Cool](https://bleedingcool.com/comics/one-page-of-jim-lee-x-men-art-sold-for-over-1-3-of-a-million-last-week/)).
- **Character and moment matter more than format.** In September a quieter Uncanny X-Men #275 Savage Land page sold for $25,000 and a WildC.A.T.s #11 cover for $23,750. Those are real money, but nowhere near the pieces with Psylocke, Wolverine and big action.

The takeaway: with Lee, collectors pay for the image they remember, not just the name on the page. See all 9 Jim Lee sales we track, with a price chart, on his [artist page](https://comicartindex.com/artists/jim-lee/).
